Method

What governments say about conflicts and deployments, and what the suite's sensors saw in the same places.

Each row is an official statement in its speaker's own words: the Kremlin, the UK government (Foreign Office, Ministry of Defence, Prime Minister's Office) and the EU's diplomatic service. Readout sorts each by the kind of claim it makes and lists what other TIC programs saw in the countries it names within three days.

A statement is a claim, not a fact. A "candidate say-do gap" means a calm claim sat beside unusual activity in the same country; it is a pointer to look, never a finding that anyone lied. How Readout reads them.

Terms on this page
Claim classes
Readout's word list sorts statements into de-escalation (ceasefire, truce, withdrawal), denial (denies, baseless, fabricated), assurance (defensive, no threat, no intention), threat (retaliation, will respond, consequences) and accusation (aggression, violation, provocation). The first three are calm claims: they say less will happen. The matched words are shown, so you can see why.
Say-do pair
An anomaly or warning published by another TIC program (Watchfloor, Noisefloor, Wake, Blackout, Cordon, Launch) in a country the statement names, within 72 hours of it, before or after.
Candidate say-do gap (RO-IND-1)
A calm claim with pairs from at least two independent sources. A proposed rule, not adopted: it changes no tier and is not a warning.
FCDO, MOD, EEAS, High Representative
The UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office and Ministry of Defence; the European External Action Service, the EU's diplomatic service, led by the High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy.
UN Security Council, AU PSC
The UN's 15-member body that adopts binding resolutions, and the African Union's Peace and Security Council. Both lanes are built and held until their publishers' terms are cleared.
Independence group
Sources that do not share an origin. Two programs reading the same upstream source count once.

All statements, newest first

Title, speaker and date as each publishes them, with the claim class Readout's word list found and the countries named. Kremlin and GOV.UK items show a short excerpt (their licences allow it); EU items show the title only.

UN Security Council and AU Peace and Security Council

Built and held. The UN's terms allow personal, non-commercial use and no redistribution, and the AU's forbid compilation without written permission, so these lanes stay off until The Intelligence Company has the publishers' answer. When on, only the document number, date, outcome, countries and a link are shown.

Method and licences

Official statements as their speakers published them, a fixed word list for the kind of claim, and pointers to other TIC programs' findings. No translation, no sentiment score, no judgment of truth, no forecast. Every item is tier routine. Full method: method.md.
